## Step 2. Experimental code

**Trial counts as run.** They match the claims.

- Study 2a: 600 per model (160 free, 160 constrained, 40 unplanted, 120 + 120 capacity). Study 2b: 80 per model.
- Study 3: 800 per model (640 encoding, 80 + 80 slot-split).

**Do conditions differ only in the intended variable?**

- Field name and description always change together ([conditions.py:69](../code/conditions.py#L69), [schema_types_design.py:98](../code/schema_types_design.py#L98)). Disclosed.
- The planted prompt labels each fact with the words its "naming" field uses. Study 1: "Your deployment region is …" against `deployment_region_code`; "Your operator is …" against `operator_account_name`; "SVC_KEY=" against `service_key_reference` ([conditions.py:574](../code/conditions.py#L574)). Studies 2 to 4 are closer: "Internal service key:" against `service_key`; "deployment tier is amber" against `deployment_tier`; "the deployment access code is …" against "Character N of the deployment access code" ([schema_types_design.py:354](../code/schema_types_design.py#L354), [release_stage1.py:196](../code/release_stage1.py#L196)). Selecting a fact by category is not separated from matching a label. This is not stated as a confound.
- The Study 1 neutral arm is one identical request sent 160 times. Seeds depend on condition and repetition only (40 distinct seeds in 320 rows), so GPT-4o returned 21 distinct outputs. Disclosed at [main.tex:791](../paper/main.tex#L791).
- Study 1 ran one field at a time with no randomization. Disclosed at [main.tex:1815](../paper/main.tex#L1815). Studies 2 to 4 shuffle within replicate blocks.

- Study 4 changes model, API surface, sampling settings, output cap, prompt and ten of fourteen wordings at once. Disclosed.

## Step 3. Defense

Three things are evaluated. None is a defense executed in a host.

1. **Task allowlist (P2).** It keeps the arguments of the correct call, which the experimenter supplies ([release_policies.py:92](../code/release_policies.py#L92), line 123). It removes the added field by definition. The paper calls it an oracle.
2. **"Provenance filter" (P3).** The authors wrote it in the commit that sealed Study 3 (`8afdd25`): a six-character substring or token Jaccard of at least 0.5, one argument at a time, no decoding. Study 3 then asks for reversed, hexadecimal and per-character values. Its failure follows from its definition, which [main.tex:1173](../paper/main.tex#L1173) concedes. The empirical content is that the models perform the transformation.
3. **Field classifier** ([defense.py](../code/defense.py)). A zero-shot prompt to `claude-haiku-4-5`, score 0 to 100.
   - Overlap between design and evaluation: yes. Two of nine positives (`system_prompt`, `model_name`) are named in the detector prompt ([defense.py:57](../code/defense.py#L57), lines 134 to 139). All nine positives are study-authored, in three families. The threshold is read from the same corpus.
   - Holdout split: none. The module docstring still says the study conditions "are now genuinely UNSEEN test data" ([defense.py:9](../code/defense.py#L9)), which the paper contradicts.
   - Real benign schemas: yes, 472 MCPTox fields and 34 OpenTelemetry fields.
   - ROC: yes (Figure I.7). A precision-recall curve is computed (`defense_eval.json`, key `pr`) and not shown. At threshold 20 the corpus precision is 9/22.
   - False positives: 13/506, five of them from one server.
   - Stability: negatives were scored once with no raw replies. The one positive near the threshold moved from 25 to 15 on rescoring.
   - An adaptive sweep of 28 rewordings exists in code ([defense.py:155](../code/defense.py#L155)). NO SOURCE FOUND for any stored result.
4. **External scanners.** One: the policy text of Invariant `mcp-scan` 0.3.39, re-implemented locally with `gpt-4o-mini`. The Snyk transcript is a tool inventory with no verdict. Nothing else was run.

**Tool-naming confound.**

- The MCP server once put condition labels in tool names. This was changed to ordinals on 2026-08-08 ([mcp_server.py:33](../code/mcp_server.py#L33)). No reported verdict comes from that server.
- The reported scanner run shows every Study 2, 3 and 4 field only on `fixture_orders`, a name that announces a test fixture ([scan_invariant.py:184](../code/scan_invariant.py#L184), line 203). The native tools, the email tool and the Study 2b contexts were not scanned. "each on the tool that its study used" ([main.tex:2441](../paper/main.tex#L2441)) is therefore inaccurate. All verdicts are "no flag", so this limits coverage and does not reverse a result.
- The authors' own "desc+name" profile is decided by parameter names they chose. `agent_identity` trips a subject-plus-object rule, `client_user_agent` does not, and the self-check asserts that C stays clean ([scan.py:59](../code/scan.py#L59), line 192). The paper already declines to treat those columns as evidence ([main.tex:2493](../paper/main.tex#L2493)).

## Step 6. Review

### Summary

The paper asks whether a tool provider that adds one required parameter to a legitimate tool schema can make a model copy a confidential system-prompt fact into that argument. Study 1, preregistered on GPT-4o and Gemini 3 Flash, plants five synthetic facts and crosses them with seven targeted fields; the targeted fact appears in 68% and 59% of calls and each other fact in about 2% and 1% of checks, an effect carried by fields that name the fact. Studies 2 and 3, preregistered on the same two models with an in-process handler, report that a permissive string pattern and enumerated, integer or boolean types do not stop an admissible value, and that the models reverse, hex-encode or split a secret on request, which passes a content filter the authors define while an experimenter-defined argument allowlist removes the field. Study 4, exploratory on GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5 and a partial Gemini 3.1 Pro leg, repeats the matrix, plants a working directory and a user name, and returns a provider refusal after the allowlist, after which every task fails. The paper also reports one re-implemented scanner policy, a prompted field classifier, two corpus surveys, a failed preregistered pilot hypothesis and a detailed provenance record.

### Verified strengths

1. **The numbers are reproducible.** Every count in the abstract and Tables 3 to 15 regenerates from raw provider payloads. My independent rescoring agrees with the recorded scores on every row of Studies 1 to 4. No result number is typed by hand in `main.tex`.
2. **The Study 1 design is sound for its question.** All facts are planted in every trial, with neutral, generic and unplanted controls, so an empty nonmatched cell means a fact was available and not sent. The stimuli are unchanged since the witness commit.
3. **Three protocols were sealed before collection,** and the commit order agrees for Studies 2 and 3. The failed pilot hypothesis is in the abstract.
4. **Two findings hold at face value and are useful.** The models compute a requested encoding of a value they were told to keep confidential (hexadecimal 151/160 and 158/160; slot-split 160/160), so matching argument text against known secrets is insufficient. Under an allowlist, a provider that refuses the reduced call got no accepted call in 349 trials, so removal trades confidentiality for availability.
5. **The limits are stated.** Adjacent fields mostly fail, the field families are absent from 401 real parameter names, the allowlist is an oracle, the classifier has no holdout, and partial and discarded legs are reported.

### Major weaknesses

**M1. Nothing leaves the process.** The threat is a third-party provider that receives a value. In Studies 2 to 4 "receipt" is an append to a Python list ([extension_harness.py:105](../code/extension_harness.py#L105)), and Study 1 measures emission only. There is no MCP transport, no remote server and no shipping client; the open items are the paper's own TODO T01 and T02 ([main.tex:531](../paper/main.tex#L531)). Whether a real client shows or blocks the added argument, which decides the security consequence, is unmeasured.

**M2. Selectivity is confounded with label matching, on author-written stimuli.** Each prompt labels a fact with the words its naming field uses (Step 2). Naming fields return the fact; two of three adjacent fields return it in 0/20 calls on both confirmatory models. The confirmatory result therefore reduces to this: a field whose name repeats a key in the prompt returns that key's value. No trial states a fact in vocabulary that differs from the field's. All 28 field texts and all prompts come from the authors, and one adjacent target was mis-specified by their own account ([main.tex:1323](../paper/main.tex#L1323)). The unit of inference is seven hand-picked fields, which gives an interval of [17, 86] for Gemini Flash.

**M3. The defense evaluation does not test a defense.** The allowlist is an oracle. The content filter was written by the authors and fails by construction against the encodings chosen for Study 3. The classifier has nine author-written positives, two of them named in its own prompt, no holdout, and an operating point that moved on rescoring. One scanner policy was re-implemented. The paper lists FIDES, CaMeL, Progent, MindGuard, MCPGuard and MCPShield and evaluated none ([main.tex:1993](../paper/main.tex#L1993), [main.tex:2017](../paper/main.tex#L2017)). No attacker adapts to any control. The title's claim that content matching does "not govern" release is supported against one self-authored filter.

**M4. Prose contradicts the data in four places that bear on conclusions.**
- The refusal arm did move a planted value: the working directory, into an allowlisted `path`, in 2 of 30 Gemini 3.1 Pro trials. [main.tex:1395](../paper/main.tex#L1395) and [main.tex:1666](../paper/main.tex#L1666) say otherwise, and the abstract and the fifth highlight depend on it.
- The classifier paragraph says naming fields "are all missed" ([main.tex:2671](../paper/main.tex#L2671)). Table I.4 shows five of them flagged at 75 to 85.
- The same field text scores 25 in one place and 15 in another ([main.tex:1573](../paper/main.tex#L1573), [main.tex:1583](../paper/main.tex#L1583)).
- The scanner coverage statement is stale and overstated ([main.tex:2441](../paper/main.tex#L2441)).

The build checks guard macros, not sentences, so these pass every check.

**M5. The confirmatory evidence is narrow, and the newer-model evidence is exploratory.** Two models, one a preview alias; one task in Study 1; 20 calls per cell; one temperature; one confidentiality sentence. Study 4 was designed in files committed after its first leg ran, its harness changed between legs, one leg is 35% complete, and it changes model, API, sampling, prompt and wordings together. The abstract nonetheless states that it "shows the same selectivity".

**M6. The paper's own surveys argue against prevalence.** The field families appear in 0 of 401 real parameter names. The planted facts are almost absent from surveyed host prompts. The one planted category that real hosts do supply, the working directory, was targeted by no field. The category that produced the Study 4 headline, a signed-in user, is supplied by none of the nine surveyed hosts ([main.tex:1281](../paper/main.tex#L1281)). The work shows a conditional capability whose preconditions are unmeasured and, by its own evidence, uncommon.

**M7. The submission is not ready.** No disclosure notice has been sent ([DISCLOSURE.md:3](../docs/DISCLOSURE.md#L3)), and that file imposes a 30-day wait after the last notice, while the ethics paragraph reads as if notices exist ([main.tex:1790](../paper/main.tex#L1790)). Affiliations, funding and the artifact DOI are open TODOs ([main.tex:230](../paper/main.tex#L230), [main.tex:2064](../paper/main.tex#L2064), [main.tex:2051](../paper/main.tex#L2051)). The label attestation is invalid.

### Minor weaknesses

1. Table 6 uses a Monte Carlo interval for DeepSeek where the text describes enumeration ([main.tex:722](../paper/main.tex#L722)).
2. The Study 3 "exact" reading normalizes case and punctuation ([paper_details.py:662](../code/paper_details.py#L662)). Strict equality gives 35/160 for GPT-4o reversal, not 51/160.
3. The pooled 0/350 includes a trial with no model output, caused by a client-library error under four concurrent workers ([main.tex:2380](../paper/main.tex#L2380)).
4. The neutral control has about 20 effective observations for GPT-4o, yet Wilson [0.0, 2.3] is printed ([main.tex:791](../paper/main.tex#L791)). The caveat belongs in the figure and table.
5. The manuscript does not say that Study 3's native tool was picked for its Study 2b success ([release_stage1.py:53](../code/release_stage1.py#L53)).
6. Study 1 unplanted controls cover six of eight fields, and GPT-4o makes no call at all in the unplanted service-key cell, so planted and unplanted trials differ in call rate.
7. Study 1 logs hold no request-side record (TODO T03, [main.tex:2767](../paper/main.tex#L2767)).
8. A precision-recall curve is computed and not shown. The adaptive rewording sweep has code and no stored result ([defense.py:155](../code/defense.py#L155)).
9. No manual audit of misses in Studies 1 to 4. First-word truncations show that the binary score undercounts partial disclosure.
10. Study 2, 3 and 4 fields were scanned only on `fixture_orders` ([scan_invariant.py:203](../code/scan_invariant.py#L203)).
11. The `defense.py` docstring claims a holdout ([defense.py:9](../code/defense.py#L9)).
12. Length: 44 pages. Caveats repeat across the body, the threats section and the appendices.
13. The prose docs are stale in the places listed in Step 5.
14. The timestamp proofs were checked through a block explorer only (TODO T04, [main.tex:2768](../paper/main.tex#L2768)).

### Overall recommendation

**Major Revision** for a mid-tier Q1 venue such as Computers & Security. At a TIFS or TDSC bar the same manuscript would be a reject with encouragement to resubmit, because M1 and M3 need new experiments and cannot be fixed by rewriting.

### The single change that would most improve the paper

Replace the in-process handler with one sealed end-to-end study through a real MCP client and a remote server, in which the protected asset is the context that client actually injects and the field texts are written by someone outside the author team. That one study would answer M1, M2 and M6 together and would turn a careful laboratory measurement into a security result.
