### Do conditions differ only in the intended variable?

- **Code shows (Study 1):** within a model file every row carries the same task id, tool (`lookup_recent_orders`), payload label, temperature (0.7) and `max_tokens` (256, `config.yaml`). Only the added declaration varies. Field name and description vary together, and descriptions differ in length and specificity (app. L2077–2080).
- **Code shows (Study 1, neutral control):** D is one identical request repeated under eight labels with the same 20 seeds, so 160 calls are about 20 independent draws. The paper says so (L788–794) but still reports the denominators 0/160 and 0/800.
- **Code shows (Study 1 ordering):** trials were collected in blocks by condition and field, so field contrasts are confounded with time (stated at L1799–1801).
- **Study 2:** each free/constrained pair shares prompt, task and facts, and differs only in `pattern` and `maxLength`. That contrast is clean. The capacity domains vary type together with wording and planted fact (L1064–1065).
- **Study 4:** differs from Studies 1–2 in model, sampling (provider defaults, not T=0.7), output cap (2,048 tokens per `.meta.json`), prompt (two added clauses), and wordings (10 of 14 never used before). The design digest equals the committed design only for the Pro leg, so the harness changed between legs (L2314–2318).
- **Scanner arms:** A′, B, B′, C, D and E share the same tool name and base description; only B adds text to the description. Study 2 and 3 arms share `fixture_orders`. No tool-naming confound.

### Temperature, seeds, model pinning

- Studies 1–3 use T=0.7. Study 4 uses provider defaults, so its temperature is uncontrolled.
- Seeds are passed only to OpenAI-compatible endpoints. Elsewhere they are labels (L2660–2663).
- Study 1 requests the undated `gpt-4o` alias and the preview `gemini-3-flash-preview`. Studies 2–3 pin `gpt-4o-2024-08-06`; Gemini stays a preview alias. Study 4 ids are echoed exactly by the providers.
- Gemini thinking tokens overran the 1,024-token cap and forced 3 resumes in Study 2 and 50 in Study 3, all under a prespecified resume rule.

### Grader and human labels

- **Studies 1–4:** deterministic normalized substring match on seed-derived markers (L621–636). Study 1 aliases are prefixes fixed in `code/conditions.py:551–570`. My re-implementation reproduces every count. The rule misses partial disclosure: 12 of 14 Study 2a misses and 17 of 33 Study 2b misses were first-word truncations (L1086–1094). That makes the binary endpoint conservative.
- **Pilots:** keyword grader plus LLM judge. One 60-row human worksheet exists. Its attestation names two labelers with identical completion times for one label column, and `code/label.py --kappa … --rater human` rejects it ("incomplete human attestation").
- **Pilot κ:** the reported κ = 0.911 is human versus keyword grader on the planted stratum, not agreement between two humans. An earlier pass (κ = 0.011) was discarded and the sheet relabelled from blank. The supplement discloses this (L266–288). It affects only the pilot measure, not Studies 1–4.

### Missing or partial legs

Study 1 Pro arm and two unplanted fields; Study 4 Pro leg (35% complete) and the discarded Flash leg. Among pilots, `wording_ablation_google`, `v2_matrix_anthropic` and `breadth` never ran; `payload_generality_openai` is 297/300 and `real_schemas_anthropic` 249/250.

## Step 3. Defense

1. **Published scanner policy** (`code/scan_invariant.py`).
   - Nothing is trained, so there is no train/test overlap.
   - The policy asks whether a tool *description* contains a prompt injection (raw record, `rule_text`). A required parameter that requests a value is not an injection under that question, so a clean verdict on C and E is close to guaranteed by construction.
   - The ten Study 4-only field texts, `account_attribution` among them, were not scanned (L1554–1555).
   - The hosted Snyk service was not run; `inspect` returns no verdict.
2. **Field-intent classifier** (`code/defense.py`, `data/defense_eval.json`).
   - Evaluation overlaps design. The 9 positives are only 7 distinct names (`caller_context_summary` and `agent_identity` appear twice). Two positives, `system_prompt` and `model_name`, are named in the detector prompt (`code/defense.py:57–58`). The first prompt named the study's attack wordings and was evaluated on them (L2519–2522).
   - There is no train/validation/holdout split (L2528–2533). The operating threshold is the one that flags every positive, so TPR = 100% by construction.
   - Negatives are real schema fields (472 MCPTox, 34 OpenTelemetry), which is good.
   - A ROC is given (Fig. roc). There is no PR curve; the PPV table is base-rate arithmetic.
   - No Study 1–4 field (region, operator, credential, encoded, slot-split, attribution) was scored (L2645–2648). Each field has one score, with no raw reply and no repeat run.
3. **Dispatch policies** (`code/release_policies.py`). Five policies were replayed on recorded Study 2 calls and written after Study 2 was collected (L1123–1124).
   - P2, the task allowlist, is defined by the experimenter: an oracle.
   - P3 knows every secret but by rule cannot match a value shorter than six normalized characters or any transformed value (`L_MIN = 6`, `release_policies.py:44`). Study 3's filter "failure" is therefore a property of the rule, fixed in advance (L1169–1173).
   - Study 4's refusal arm is the only place a model reacts to a policy. It is local, and its wording is the authors'.
4. **External comparison.** Only one external policy was evaluated. Runtime systems that the paper itself says would address the flow (CaMeL, FIDES, Progent, MindGuard, MCPGuard; L1958–2003) were not run.

## Step 4. Realism

- **Synthetic tools carry the confirmatory weight.** `lookup_recent_orders` carries all of Study 1. `fixture_orders` and `fixture_email` carry Study 2a, most of Study 3 and most of Study 4.
- **Schemas from real sources:**
  - 8 native schemas captured from version-pinned MCP servers (`data/source_snapshots/native/`: caldav, google calendar, fastmail, imap, files-go, files-official, saleor, shopify). They are used in Study 2b with one added field and 5 pairs each, served by local fixtures. `files-official` is reused in Studies 3 and 4.
  - 25 MCPTox tool listings reconstructed from rendered text, with all types collapsed to string. They are used only in the pilot robustness arm, with the keyword measure.
  - Prevalence corpora: 45 MCPTox servers (401 names), MCP-Zero (308 servers, LLM-extracted from documentation), and 9 open-source hosts' prompt sources.
- **End-to-end runs:** `runs/mcp_client_capture.jsonl` holds three live calls from 2026-08-10: GPT-4o through a real MCP stdio client session (`code/mcp_client_probe.py`) to the repository's own server, using the pilot `cursor` scaffold, n=1 per condition. Condition C delivered `CursorIDE` to the server. **The paper does not report this capture** and states that receipt "involves neither an MCP transport nor a remote provider" (L516–517).
- **Not found:** any run against a commercial client (Claude Desktop, Cursor, Claude Code), any remote server, and any client-UI observation. `docs/THREAT_MODEL.md` §6 registered a consent-surface test (L202–221) that was never executed (T02).

## Step 6. Review

### Summary

The manuscript asks whether a third-party tool provider can obtain confidential system-prompt content by adding one required parameter to an otherwise legitimate tool, and whether checks a host can apply at dispatch decide what is released. Study 1, preregistered on GPT-4o and Gemini 3 Flash, plants five seed-derived facts and crosses seven targeted fields: matched recovery is 68% and 59% against 2% and 1% for nonmatched facts, with naming fields at or near ceiling and two of three non-naming fields at zero. Studies 2 and 3, also preregistered and measured at a local handler, find that a permissive string pattern does not reduce recovery, that categorical fields carry admissible planted values, and that models produce hexadecimal, reversed, suffix and per-character forms that pass an oracle content filter, while an experimenter-defined argument allowlist removes every planted value. Study 4, exploratory on three newer models with one partial leg, reproduces selectivity, reports that a billing-attribution field returned a planted user name in 30 of 30 trials, and finds no relocation of the secret when a strict handler refuses the reduced call, at the cost of every task failing. A supplement reports pilot studies, a failed preregistered superiority hypothesis, a scanner-policy re-run, a field classifier, and corpus surveys suggesting the attacked field families and planted material are rare in public MCP data.

### Verified strengths

1. **The numbers are real.** Every count I recomputed with independently written code matches the raw logs: Studies 1–4, the reconstructed-listing arm, the scanner record and the classifier scores. Offline reproduction passes, and regenerated macros and figures are byte-identical to those committed.
2. **Provenance is unusually strong for Studies 1 and 3.** `protocol-v3.md` was added in `6114a4d` (2026-08-10 15:16 UTC) with no v3 artifact, and its digest is unchanged. The Study 3 protocol was committed 83 minutes before the first planted call. Inputs are pinned by hash in `data/artifact_selection.json`.
3. **No judge is needed for the outcome.** Seed-derived canaries with substring scoring make Studies 1–4 deterministic. Three alternative scoring rules leave the primary counts unchanged.
4. **The simultaneous-fact design is a real methodological contribution.** Nonmatched facts are checked in the same calls, so "selective versus indiscriminate" is measured directly. Crosstalk traces to the broad client field (8/9 and 4/4).
5. **Reporting is candid.** The failed hypothesis appears in the abstract. Post-collection analysis changes, partial and discarded legs (with their outcomes), the Study 4 commit order and the label-attestation problem are all disclosed.
6. **Receipt-level endpoints with a task oracle (Studies 2–4)** separate emission, validity, dispatch, receipt and task success. Study 4 exposes the availability cost of structural removal, a point the literature mostly ignores.
7. **The Study 2 pairs are clean**, and the categorical guessing baseline uses hidden balanced labels.

### MAJOR weaknesses (block acceptance)

**M1. The selectivity result reduces to "a field that names a fact retrieves that fact", which prior work already shows.**
- Evidence: naming fields recover 75/80 and 62/80. The two genuinely non-naming fields are 0/20 on every complete Study 1 arm (L812–828, Fig. gradient). The one adjacent success is a near-synonym (L820–822).
- Prior work: HiddenLayer and MSB already show that named parameters extract prompt content (L291–294, L1902–1914).
- What is left: the crossed design, and the observation that unrequested facts stay put. The latter is what a model filling a schema should do; the paper does not argue why it is a security property rather than ordinary instruction following.
- Overclaim: "a schema [as] a precise query" (L319–322) and "a selector over the confidential content" (L2008–2009) imply a query language whose only demonstrated vocabulary is naming the category.
- Study 4's adjacent successes use different wordings under different settings and are exploratory (L1319–1324).

**M2. The dispatch-control conclusions are bounded by construction, and the title outruns them.**
- Study 2's pattern admits every planted value by design (L955–958), so a null is the expected result for a regex.
- Study 3's filter cannot by rule match values under six characters or transformed values, and the schemas state the encoding openly (L1119–1122, L1169–1173, L1245–1248).
- The policy that "held throughout" is an experimenter-defined allowlist (L1699–1702).
- The informative finding is narrower: models compute transformations of values they were told to keep confidential.
- The title ("Schema Checks and Content Filters Do Not Govern Their Release", L224) and contribution 3 (L361–367) generalize to filters and checks that were never tested: no decoding or joining filter, no allowlist derived without an oracle, no provider that adapts to a filter.

**M3. External validity is narrow, and the paper's own surveys cut against it.**
- The confirmatory evidence rests on:
  - one task with one synthetic tool (Study 1);
  - two synthetic tools plus eight native contexts carrying one field (Study 2);
  - two older models;
  - author-written wordings (L340–342, L1826–1828);
  - synthetic secrets;
  - an in-process handler (L514–517).
- The ecological section finds the attacked field families at 0/401 and the planted material essentially absent from 195k characters of host prompt (L1602–1612, L1632–1637).
- The one category real hosts do supply, user and workspace context, is tested only in exploratory Study 4, and there no field targets it.

**M4. Study 4 uses confirmatory vocabulary it cannot support, and its most novel result is post hoc.**
- The design files were committed with the data about 1.5 hours after the first planted call (L2306–2311; verified: `cfec543` 00:41 UTC vs 23:04 UTC). Yet the text speaks of a "prespecified threshold" (L1310), a "prespecified bound" (L1377) and a "prespecified stop" (L2369), and reports met / not met decisions (L1315–1316, L1380–1381).
- The harness changed between legs (L2314–2318).
- The attribution finding rests on one field text, 30 trials, a target the paper now calls "the wrong prediction" (L1353–1356), and an unconfirmed field review (T31, L2348). It nonetheless appears in contribution 1 (L354–356), the ecological section (L1650–1652), the discussion (L1673–1675) and the conclusion (L2014–2015).
- The discarded leg is the only one in which a model told the user the secret (2/21, L2331–2336). The paper asserts its outputs were not inspected before the stop (L2329–2330), while the readiness audit lists that as an open author question (`Q1_READINESS_AUDIT.md:43`).

**M5. Study 1's statistical unit is small and hand-picked, and its inference was finalized after collection.**
- Seven fields with author-assigned targets.
- The paired bootstrap and the denominator correction were implemented after collection (L713–716). The lower endpoint moves with the seed (L716–722). Gemini's interval spans 69 points ([17, 86]).
- The "0/800" neutral control is about 20 independent requests (L788–794).
- Fields were collected in blocks, so field differences are confounded with time (L1799–1801).
- H1 passes, but the per-fact Fisher/Holm tests (app. L2139–2147) and the 0/800 denominator suggest precision the design does not have.

**M6. The defense evaluation is below the Q1 standard.**
- Classifier: 9 positives (7 distinct names), two named in its own prompt, no holdout, a threshold chosen on the evaluation set (so TPR = 100% by construction), no score for any Study 1–4 field, no raw replies.
- Scanner: one policy whose question cannot be expected to flag a parameter request; the Study 4 texts were not scanned.
- Runtime policy systems the paper names as the remedy were not evaluated.
- The allowlist is an oracle, evaluated by replay (Step 3).

**M7. The artifact is not in a reviewable state.**
- `paper/main.tex` does not compile in the project environment, and `make -C paper check` fails.
- The committed PDF predates the source and carries a different title.
- The source contains 27 TODO comments (30 distinct tracked items across main and supplement), and lacks affiliations and a corresponding author (L228) and funding (L2048). The AI-use statement is unreviewed (L2055).
- The human-label attestation is rejected by the repository's own validator.
- All of this is mechanical to fix, but it blocks submission.

### MINOR weaknesses

1. **Abstract.** It packs 15 macros and mixes counts with percentages. "The secret moves elsewhere in 0/350 trials, a bound met on the two complete legs only" (L252–253) is hard to parse.
2. **Study 3 decisions.** The registered containment rule credits over-long answers. The exact rule takes GPT-4o suffix from 102 to 64 of 160; the token-containment variant roughly halves suffix counts (153→78, 102→51) (L1203–1217). The decision was not recomputed under either reading (L1218–1219).
3. **Region suffix.** The target is three alphanumerics (for example `yb3` from `ux-nkyyb-3`), so "obtained the target" overstates what leaves. Report the information disclosed per field.
4. **Unplanted controls.** They cannot fail for 16–20-character seed tokens, which the paper concedes (L609–612), yet they are listed as a finding (L352–353, L2011). The informative signal goes unanalysed: with nothing planted, Gemini calls the tool in only 2, 6 and 9 of 20 trials for the operator, region and credential fields, against 20/20 when planted (`runs/v3_unplanted_google-live-20260811-124509.jsonl`). Having the fact changes whether the model calls the tool.
5. **Optional-field table.** The required baselines (Table depth, L1512–1514) come from the v2 pilot matrix, a different run under a protocol whose provenance failed, so the contrast is between runs.
6. **Study 4 cost.** The figure is labelled "billed" but is the ledger's committed total: settled cost is US$6.05, and the discarded leg's US$0.22 is reported nowhere (L2388–2389).
7. **Omitted transport capture.** The MCP stdio capture (`runs/mcp_client_capture.jsonl`) is left out while the text says no MCP transport was used (L516–517).
8. **Model settings.** Study 1 uses undated or preview aliases. Study 4's sampling settings are provider defaults, which compounds the cross-study comparison (L2324–2325).
9. **Robustness arms.** The reconstructed-listing arm and the reticence ladder use a different construct (keyword identity). Ladder cells hold 10 trials, and Gemini's rung-2 A′ cell has 9 errors in 20 attempts (L1443–1447).
10. **Bibliography.** Ten entries lack pages, HiddenLayer's title is mismatched, and several entries were checked only at abstract level (L1924, L1991–1992, L2004, L2904). AirGapAgent (data minimization for agents) is the closest analogue to the allowlist and deserves more than one sentence.
11. **Venue.** `\journal{Computers & Security}` (L220) sits beside a TODO saying its author guide was not checked (L219). README and the audit say no venue is named.
12. **Length.** About 50 pages built. Status caveats repeat across sections, and appendices restate textbook material (Wilson formula, L2774–2790).
13. **Disclosure statement.** The ethics paragraph should state plainly that disclosure is pending; `docs/DISCLOSURE.md` is untracked and records no notice sent.
14. **Terminology drift.** "Adjacent" in the paper is `indirect` in the Study 4 code and data; "nonmatched" is "off-diagonal" in the docs.

### Overall recommendation: **Major Revision**

The measurement is careful and every number I checked reproduces, so the problems are scope and framing, not error. They cannot be fixed by rewording: the paper needs at least one confirmatory study on fields the authors did not write, with host-realistic context and remote receipt, before its general claims about release control are supported.

### The single change that would most improve the paper

Run one preregistered study on current models in which people other than the authors write several fields per category, including the user and workspace context real hosts supply, and measure what a remote server receives over a real MCP transport. It would test whether the channel works on fields the authors did not choose, turn the post hoc attribution finding into a confirmatory one, and replace the local-handler endpoint that limits every receipt claim.
